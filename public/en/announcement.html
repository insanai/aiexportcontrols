01. What changed, and when¶

One announcement, two clocks¶

MOFCOM and the Ministry of Science and Technology issued Announcement 48 on 16 October 2026. The first clock concerns a proposed change to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted from Export. The second concerns temporary registration before covered exports. Registration began on publication. The proposed prohibitions and licensing categories have not become final merely because the registration duty has started. CN48, sections I and III

The consultation runs from 16 October through 15 November 2026. The temporary registration period ends when the revised catalogue is formally issued and implemented. The announcement gives no fixed calendar date for that event. We found no final replacement in the sources checked for this edition. A later announcement could change this account. CN48, sections II and III

What counts as a transfer¶

Registration covers transferring ownership abroad, licensing domestic technology for overseas use, and a domestic technology owner or actual controller becoming an overseas entity. MOFCOM may specify further situations by notice. Exports already started but not completed are included. A company transaction can therefore matter even when no box crosses a border. That last sentence is our reading of the ownership and control provision. CN48, section III(2)

The relevant application is the technology trade information service on MOFCOM's unified platform at ecomp.mofcom.gov.cn. Registration collects exporter and recipient information, destination, technology, use and expected value. Several technologies going to the same recipient in one export may be combined. Other cases should normally be filed separately. Registration is not itself an export license or proof that another restriction has been satisfied. CN48, section III CN-A2

Why the wording matters¶

A prohibition would stop a covered export within its stated scope. A restriction would create a permission route. Automatic licensing still describes a procedure. An exclusion removes a specified situation from that particular scope. We must read each qualification before using the headline category. CN-A1, notes 1 to 4 CNQA

MOFCOM describes the revision as routine technology-trade management, not a measure aimed at a particular country, region or entity. That is the government's stated purpose. It is not evidence that the commercial effects will be the same in every market. CNQA, background question

A worked example¶

Suppose a company plans to license private model technology to an overseas partner in November. We first identify the exact technology and the relevant proposed entry. We then check whether the transfer falls within the temporary registration scope and existing law. We cannot simply wait for the consultation to end. Conversely, the draft's proposed prohibited status is not enough to claim that every such export is already banned by Announcement 48. This is an illustrative application of the two clocks, not a transaction clearance. CN48 CN-A1
