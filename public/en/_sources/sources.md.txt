# 10. Sources, method and small glossary

## How this book was researched

We read the official announcement, both downloaded WPS attachments and the official questions and answers. We compared US Federal Register rules with BIS's current published regulatory text, BIS's non-enforcement announcement and GAO's May 2026 decision. We checked Treasury and Justice Department sources for adjacent programs, and model publishers for concrete open-release examples. Research was checked on 2 November 2026.

We use short source IDs beside claims. The list below gives the original link, date and a location within the source. The atlas identifies each point by code. The research folder preserves downloaded bytes, hashes and download limitations. All 40 summaries are editorial paraphrases. They do not replace the Chinese attachment or the current US rules.

The official files are WPS documents. Our PDF reading copies were converted locally with LibreOffice. We also provide a PDF print of the official announcement page. Conversion may change pagination. The original WPS bytes and source links allow comparison. We do not relabel the unrelated user-supplied organoid PDF as Announcement 48.

## Translation and uncertainty

All seven editions cover the same chapters and 40-point atlas. Translations are editorial reading aids and have not received independent legal or native-language professional review. The original legal texts prevail. English words inside catalogue codes, product names and source titles remain where needed for verification. Diagrams simplify relationships and show no invented quantitative forecasts.

Statements about what a rule says carry sources. Hypothetical examples and possible futures are explicitly marked. We do not claim that a company will stop publishing models, that an individual transaction is approved, or that our search excludes the existence of every later legal document.

## A small glossary

**Export:** a transfer covered by a rule. It can involve knowledge or rights rather than a physical shipment.

**Weights:** the learned numerical settings of an AI model.

**Training:** the process that adjusts those settings using data and computation.

**Inference:** using a trained model to produce an answer.

**Agent:** a system that can plan steps and use tools to work toward a task.

**API:** a way for one program to ask another program for a service.

**Open weights:** model settings made available for download; the phrase alone says little about training code or data.

**License:** either a government permission or a copyright permission, depending on context. We always distinguish the two.

**Proposed prohibited / restricted:** a category in the consultation draft, not a claim that all its new final rules already operate.
