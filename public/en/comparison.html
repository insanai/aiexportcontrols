08. What the comparison tells us¶

Compare the gates, not the slogans¶

China's Announcement 48 focuses on technology leaving China, including ownership and control changes. The US framework focuses on covered items, destinations, recipients and uses, with a reach to some foreign-made items. Both can affect an AI project at different points. These are summaries of the cited systems, not claims that either country controls all technology worldwide. CN48 EAR734 EAR742 EAR744

The same project can meet both systems¶

Consider a hypothetical Chinese developer that uses covered US chips at an overseas computing center and later licenses private model technology to a foreign partner. US rules may matter to the chips, users and uses. China's proposed technology categories and current registration may matter to the model transfer. A lawful path under one system does not establish a lawful path under the other. This is an illustrative interaction, not a claim about a real company. CN48 CN-A1 EAR734 EAR744

What we can and cannot infer¶

Controls could raise documentation costs, encourage hosted services or influence where firms build factories and computing centers. Those are plausible mechanisms. The announcement alone does not quantify their effect on prices, jobs, investment, clean-energy adoption or AI performance. We make no numerical forecast. Evidence for an outcome would require observed transactions, implementation data and a comparison with what would have happened without the controls.

A similar number in two rules can hide different meanings. China's 10^26 test is one of several alternative advanced-model criteria. The US 4E091 entry has its own operations definition, exclusions and enforcement complication. We cannot call the systems identical because one threshold looks similar.
